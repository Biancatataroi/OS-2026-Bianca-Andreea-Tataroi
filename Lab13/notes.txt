Operating Systems — Laboratory 13
Semaphores in Your Own Kernel (xv6)

1. Without the lock, why do the letters mix?

The parent and child processes execute concurrently.
Each character is printed using a separate write() system call.
Since each line contains 30 letters and a newline,
printing one line requires 31 system calls.
The operating system can switch between processes,
causing their characters to mix.

2. With the lock, every line is whole. Which two lines
of mix.c make one line a critical section?

sem_down(3);
sem_up(3);

sem_down(3) acquires the semaphore before printing.
sem_up(3) releases it after printing.
This ensures that only one process prints a line at a time.

3. Why does sem_down use while instead of if?

A process may wake up even when the semaphore value is 0.
Another process might acquire the semaphore first.
The while loop checks the value again before proceeding.

4. Why does sem_down release the spinlock before sleep()?

A process must not sleep while holding a spinlock.
Otherwise, another process may be unable to acquire
the spinlock and execute sem_up().
